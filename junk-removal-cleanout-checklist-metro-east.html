The Metro East & St. Louis Junk Removal Cleanout Checklist (2026)

Whole-house cleanouts are a different job than a single-item pickup. Here's how to plan one — and who to call — whether you're in Granite City, Belleville, or the St. Louis city core.

A full home cleanout — after a downsize, a foreclosure, an estate, or just years of accumulation — is one of the most physically demanding projects a homeowner takes on, and it's rarely a one-crew job. This checklist breaks it into stages so nothing gets missed and nothing gets hauled twice.

1. Walk the Property and Triage by Room

Before any hauler shows up, walk every room and flag: furniture staying, furniture donating, and furniture that's genuinely trash (water-damaged, broken, or beyond repair). This turns a vague "clean it all out" quote into a specific, faster job.

2. Book the Right Hauler for the Volume

Metro East has several operators who specialize in full-property cleanouts rather than curbside single-item pickups — worth confirming that distinction on the call:

  • Pro Junk Dispatch — Granite City-based, whole-property and estate cleanout crews.
  • Bro's Hauling — general hauling with same-day availability in the Metro East corridor.
  • Sparks Junk Removal and Hauling — full-service removal covering both Illinois and Missouri sides of the metro.

3. Separate Donation-Eligible Items First

Furniture, working appliances, and household goods in good condition should go to donation before the hauler arrives — most junk removal crews will happily skip donation-tagged items to save you the fee, but only if it's flagged in advance.

4. Confirm Disposal Compliance

Illinois and Missouri both regulate certain waste streams (electronics, paint, appliances with refrigerant) differently from general junk. Before a large cleanout, it's worth a quick check with your municipality:

  • Illinois Department of Natural Resources — Illinois-side disposal and recycling guidance for Metro East residents.
  • Missouri Department of Natural Resources — Missouri-side household hazardous waste and electronics disposal rules.

5. Deep Clean After the Last Load Leaves

Once the junk is out, the space still needs a real clean before it's move-in or list-ready — dust, grime, and buildup sit under and behind everything that just got hauled away. For St. Louis-side properties, Clean Town & Country's move-out cleaning handles this final pass with a steam-first, 275°F thermal shock deep-clean protocol built for post-cleanout turnovers.

Bottom line: sequence the cleanout — triage, donate, haul, verify disposal rules, then deep clean — and a project that feels chaotic on day one wraps in a weekend.
