The St. Louis Homeowner's Guide to Estate Liquidation & Downsizing (2026)

Clearing out a family home — after a move, a downsize, or a loss — is one of the most overwhelming projects a St. Louis homeowner ever takes on. Here's how locals actually do it, step by step.

Whether you're helping a parent downsize from a longtime Kirkwood home, settling an estate in South City, or simply clearing decades of belongings before a sale, estate liquidation in St. Louis follows a fairly predictable path once you know the steps. This guide walks through the process from first sort to final clean.

Step 1: Sort Before You Call Anyone

Before bringing in a liquidator, do a rough first pass. Separate items into keep, sell, donate, and discard piles. This single step saves liquidators and haulers hours of billable time and gets you a more accurate quote.

Step 2: Get the Estate Appraised and Sold

For homes with genuine collectibles, antiques, or a full household of furniture, a professional estate sale or auction house will get more value than a garage sale. St. Louis has a deep bench of specialists here:

  • Selkirk Auctioneers & Appraisers — full-service estate auctions and appraisals.
  • St. Louis Estate Buyers — direct buyout option when a fast, single-transaction sale is preferred over a multi-day estate sale.
  • Asgoodasnew Auction Co. — online estate auction format, good for smaller households or long-distance heirs coordinating remotely.

Get quotes from at least two before committing — commission structures vary widely (flat fee vs. percentage of sale).

Step 3: Haul What Doesn't Sell

After the sale (or if there wasn't enough for a formal sale), leftover furniture, appliances, and general household debris need to go. Metro East and St. Louis both have established junk removal operators who specialize in whole-house cleanouts, not just single-item pickups — worth asking specifically about estate cleanout experience when you call.

Step 4: Handle Probate Paperwork Early

If the property is going through probate, don't wait on the liquidation to start the paperwork — the two tracks can run in parallel. The Missouri Courts probate self-help center has forms and county-specific guidance, and St. Louis County residents can check requirements directly with the St. Louis County Department of Revenue, Real Estate Division before listing or transferring the property.

Step 5: Final Deep Clean Before Listing or Handoff

Once the home is empty, it needs a genuine deep clean — not a quick wipe-down — before it goes on the market or gets handed to the next owner. This is the step most estates underestimate: an empty house shows every scuff, dust layer, and forgotten corner that furniture used to hide.

That's the point where Clean Town & Country's move-out cleaning service comes in — a steam-first, 275°F thermal shock deep-clean built specifically for empty-home turnovers, so the property shows well from day one.

Bottom line: estate liquidation in St. Louis is a four-part relay — sort, sell, haul, clean — and each leg has a specialist worth hiring rather than DIY-ing. Line them up in order and the whole process compresses from months to weeks.
