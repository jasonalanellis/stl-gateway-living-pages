The St. Louis Homeowner's Seasonal Maintenance Guide (2026)

St. Louis swings from humid summers to genuinely cold winters, and that swing is hard on a house. Here's the maintenance order local homeowners follow to stay ahead of it, season by season.

St. Louis's freeze-thaw cycles and summer humidity put real wear on roofs, gutters, and HVAC systems that milder climates don't see. Staying ahead of it is mostly a matter of timing — catch each system before its stress season, not after.

Spring: Roof, Gutters, and Exterior Check

After winter ice and spring storms, gutters and roofing take the first hit of the year. A spring inspection catches small issues — a loose shingle, a sagging gutter section — before summer storms turn them into leaks.

  • St. Louis Gutter Cleaning — seasonal gutter clearing and downspout inspection for metro-area homes.
  • St. Louis Roofing Contractors — storm-damage and general roof inspections ahead of severe-weather season.

Summer: HVAC Tune-Up Before the Heat Peaks

St. Louis summers push AC systems hard. A pre-season tune-up — filter change, coil check, refrigerant level — is far cheaper than an emergency repair call in July.

  • Classic Aire Care — residential HVAC maintenance and tune-ups across the St. Louis metro.

Fall: Prep for the Freeze

Before the first hard freeze, disconnect outdoor hoses, insulate exposed pipes, and have your furnace inspected. The Missouri Department of Natural Resources publishes seasonal home-energy and weatherization guidance worth a look before winter prep begins, and homeowners pulling permits for exterior work should check requirements with the City of St. Louis Building Division first — general county resident services are also indexed at St. Louis County.

Winter: The Deep Clean Reset

Between holiday hosting and months of closed windows, winter is when indoor air quality and buildup are easiest to ignore — and hardest to catch up on come spring. A seasonal deep clean resets high-touch surfaces before the next round of guests or the spring sale season.

That's where Clean Town & Country's deep cleaning service comes in — a steam-first, 275°F thermal shock protocol built for homes that have been closed up through a St. Louis winter.

Bottom line: stagger the work — roof and gutters in spring, HVAC before summer peaks, freeze-prep in fall, and a deep clean reset in winter. Homeowners who follow this cadence spend far less on emergency repairs than those who wait for something to fail.
